# Auraliscope 2.0 beta (build 106)

Build 106 updates the 2.0 beta. Install it over build 92 to 105: the app and the Bridge plugins are replaced in place,
and settings stay as they are. Install the app and the plugins together: a plugin before build 98 keeps working, but
only with the standard speaker layout.

## New in build 106

- **Japanese, Traditional Chinese and Spanish.** Settings ▸ General ▸ Language now offers 日本語, 繁體中文 (Taiwan usage)
  and Español (España) beside English, 简体中文 and 한국어, for everything the app shows: the meter window, menus,
  Settings, the panels' settings, file analysis and the PDF report. As before, units, channel labels, standards and
  product names keep their console notation, and a language previews at once without interrupting the measurement.
  System picks the first of the six in the Mac's language list.
- **Type the target and the ceiling.** Loudness panel settings ▸ Target and True Peak ceiling take a typed value (to a
  tenth; −14, 14, −14,5 and “−14 LUFS” all read as −14 or −14.5) as well as the arrows.
- **Panel settings in groups.** Each panel's settings popover opens with the panel's name and sets its choices in
  titled cards: Loudness (Reading; Target for the mode in use), Loudness History (Plot; Lines), VU (Calibration),
  Spectrogram & Spectrum (Spectrogram; Spectrogram colours; Spectrum; Both views), Waveform (Lanes), Soundstage (Stereo;
  Immersive; the body; Camera; Show; then Analysis and motion) and Channel Levels (Peak hold; Correlation). A row whose
  name and control do not fit side by side (a long translation) sets the control under its name.

## Changed in build 106

- **Smoother Stereo ↔ Immersive.** The Soundstage hands over instead of double-exposing: the stereometer swells a little
  and fades as the dome settles in from slightly farther away (and the reverse on the way back); the nebula fades with
  the dome; the BALANCE / WIDTH readings and the BANDS menu fade with the stereometer; and the headers' mode-dependent
  words cross-fade. The canvases and their labels now start together: the canvases used to begin a frame ahead
  (counting the time the change took to lay out), so Channel Levels' bars ran ahead of their names and readings.
- **Enlarging a panel** grows it out of its place in the window to fill it while the other panels step back, and
  restoring shrinks it back into place while they return; the plots keep drawing at every size and their scales and
  readings fade back in as it lands. Before, the window went blank for a moment and the panel faded in.
- **Into and out of Dock mode** the content no longer stretches: what is leaving holds still and fades, the window
  glides to its new frame, and what is arriving fades in at its own size as the window settles. Leaving Dock mode used
  to stretch the dock's panels into tall columns for a moment before the full window appeared.
- **A wider divider range.** The divider between the analyzers (Spectrogram & Spectrum, Waveform) and the Soundstage
  column now moves from a 280 pt Soundstage column to 520 pt analyzers, with no fixed upper limit, so either side can
  take most of a large window.
- **Titles in the wordmark's voice.** Settings' page titles, Speaker Layout's title and the panel settings' titles use
  the Auraliscope wordmark's font.
- **A quieter dome.** The text notes in the Immersive Soundstage's upper right corner (phantom image, ochre, body) are
  gone; the FREQUENCY and GLOW = LEVEL / RADIUS = LEVEL keys stay. The same notes are no longer added to the one-line key
  of a narrow Soundstage either. VoiceOver still describes them.

## Fixed in build 106

- In Stereo, the Soundstage's band legend (20 · 120 · 500 Hz…) overlapped the BALANCE and WIDTH readings at the default
  column width. It now sits between them, by its numbers alone where the column is narrow.
- A report page whose notes run long (Spanish) sets its tables a little tighter instead of running off the page.

## New in build 103

- **A new look: glass, soft light and shade, and motion.** The panels are frosted glass over a deep, nearly colourless
  night (on light, a grey haze), so colour belongs to the signal and the lit controls. Light falls from the upper
  left: panels catch it along one edge, control tracks are pressed into the surface, and a round button rises out of
  its track under the pointer, sinks into it as you click and glows when it is on. Every control moves with the same
  soft springs, symbols morph and bounce as their state changes (the pin, pause and play), and the LIVE light
  breathes. With Reduce Motion on (System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display), the motion becomes short fades.
- **New colour themes.** Indigo, Sage and Starlight stay, joined by Cobalt (a clear blue), Glacier (pale ice), Lagoon
  (a deep teal), Mint (a soft green) and Graphite (cool silver, for a monochrome meter). None of them uses the meter's
  caution and alarm colours (amber and red) or the height channels' lavender. A theme that is gone moves to its nearest
  new one: Ice Blue to Glacier, Ocean & Mist to Lagoon, Phosphor Green to Mint, Electric Violet to Cobalt, Plum & Pearl
  to Indigo, and Blossom Pink, Vintage Amber, Safety Orange and Copper & Linen to Starlight.
- **SF Symbols on every button.** Buttons that showed only text (Toggle Full Screen, Reset Layout, Apply, Revert,
  Save PDF Report…, Import, Export, the panel menus and more) now carry the matching Apple symbol.

## Changed in build 103

- **Always on top starts off.** The meter window no longer floats over other apps' windows at every launch. Turn it on
  with the pin in the toolbar, View ▸ Always on Top, or Settings ▸ General ▸ Window; it stays as you leave it.
  Settings, About and Speaker Layout float only while the meter does.
- **The horizontal strip is now Dock mode** (still ⌥⌘S, Layout ▸ Dock Mode). Its button back to the full window shows an
  arrow rising out of the dock. The notes for earlier builds below still call it the strip.
- **Consistent Settings pages.** Every page sets its title, sections, rows and notes on one text edge with the same

- **As light as before.** The new look and motion cost no measurable CPU against build 102 (about 22 % of one core
  while metering, in Stereo and Immersive alike); the VU needle now sends the system one screen update less per frame.

## Changed in build 102

- **A crisper Radiant body.** Its light starts half as wide and swells less as it ages, and the body's edge is firmer,
  so sound sources read as shapes rather than haze; the point lights keep less glow around them.

## Fixed in build 102

- In Stereo, the Filled stereometer left dark wedges between the bands' shapes and the horizon: each shape was pinched
  back to the listener at both ends of the horizon. A band that reaches out to the horizon now closes along it.

## New in build 101

- **Radiant body.** The Immersive Soundstage's Radiant style is now Radiant body: each frequency band is still placed
  where it is heard (at the centre of gravity of the speakers that carry it, weighted by its level in each), with
  Radiant's analysis, detail, glow range, persistence, hover readings and raw observations, but it is drawn as the
  Level body's glowing gas instead of as points of light: one translucent volume in the frequencies' hues that burns
  toward a cream-white core where it is dense and loud, keeps its fringes coloured by what dominates there, carries
  the same fine grain, and fades out at the speakers' shell. New light is compact and swells into haze as it ages
  (Particle size, Blur). Over the body, build 100's small point lights mark exactly where each band's frequencies sit
  (Soundstage settings ▸ Points; 0 shows the body alone), and the body dims a little under them so they carry the
  brightest light. A centred phantom gathers between L and R, a wide stereo programme stretches along the line
  between them, and sound all round gathers toward the listener. Raw observations still draws the particles alone,
  for checking positions. The Sparkle setting is gone; the point lights keep build 100's glint. Whoever had chosen
  Radiant gets Radiant body; Level body is unchanged.
- **Routing from Audio MIDI Setup.** An audio device without a routing of its own in Auraliscope now follows its
  speaker configuration in Audio MIDI Setup (Configure Speakers): each channel feeds the speaker macOS has it set up
  for, in any format up to 9.1.6, and a change made there applies at once. A virtual device (Pro Tools Audio Bridge,
  BlackHole and the like) configured on its output side is read that way on its input too. Speakers the configuration
  does not describe stay unrouted rather than guessed; a device without one keeps SMPTE order as before. Settings ▸
  Channels ▸ Routing says which applies, and offers the configuration's format when the one chosen differs.
- **Channel Order menu.** Settings ▸ Channels ▸ Routing ▸ Channel Order routes every channel at once: Audio MIDI Setup,
  SMPTE / ITU (L R C LFE Ls Rs…), Film (Pro Tools: L C R Ls Rs LFE…), or VST3 (Nuendo, Cubase: L R C LFE, rear
  surrounds, side surrounds, the heights, and the wides last).

## Fixed in build 101

- A 9.1.6 programme captured from Nuendo or Cubase through an audio device was drawn with ten of its sixteen channels
  on the wrong speakers (wides as top rear, top front as wides, top rear as top front, sides and rears swapped): those
  hosts send VST3 order, which the default SMPTE order misreads. Set the device's speakers up in Audio MIDI Setup, or
  choose Channel Order ▸ VST3. The Bridge plugin was never affected: it reads each channel's speaker from the bus.

## Changed in build 100

- **Smoother motion.** Every moving display now steps by the time its frame reaches the screen, not by when it happens
  to be drawn, so motion advances by the same amount every frame. The spectrogram and waveform scroll at an even speed
  however the audio arrives: with a DAW's 1024- or 2048-sample buffers the spectrogram used to pause on more than half
  of the frames, and the waveform's speed swung by up to 30 %; both now stay within about 1 %. The LAST 15 S loudness
  history glides between its readings instead of jumping a step ten times a second, at no extra cost.
- **ProMotion.** Settings ▸ Performance ▸ Display frame rate: Automatic now follows the display, 120 fps on a ProMotion
  display and 60 on others (30 in Low Power Mode), and moves with the window between displays; 120 fps can be chosen.

## New in build 98

- **Your own speaker layout.** Soundstage settings ▸ Speaker layout opens a window to place each speaker of a channel
  format by azimuth, elevation (below the ear plane too) and, when known, distance in meters, or to leave a speaker out
  of the spatial picture. Mirror copies one side's places to the other; layouts can be duplicated, renamed, deleted,
  exported and imported as JSON files (docs/SPEAKER_LAYOUT_FORMAT.md). The dome, its speaker labels and the spatial
  analysis use the layout, in the app and in the Bridge plugins of this build; a layout with speakers below the ear
  plane shows the lower half of the dome. Routing, levels and loudness are not affected.
- **Radiant from one exact reduction.** Each particle is now one place where a band's frequencies sit in the newest
  analysis: neighbouring frequencies at the same place are combined exactly, every place that carries a good share of
  the band is kept, and the weakest rest is gathered, so each band's energy, centre of gravity and spread are kept
  whatever the Particles setting. The analysis does this once, in the plugin as in the app, and the plugin's and the
  app's particles agree.
- **Brightness no longer depends on Particles.** Until build 97 a band's light grew with the square root of its
  number of particles: on a 7.1.4 test programme the picture's total light rose 77 % from 5 to 32 particles. A band now
  emits its light once, shared by its particles in proportion to their energy, so its light and its centre of light are
  the same at any number of particles (the picture now changes by 17 % over that span, from the display's tone
  mapping alone). At the default 20 particles the picture carries about 86 % of build 97's light, its brightness
  centre within 1 % of the frame of build 97's.
- **Speaker positions.** With distances entered, Radiant can place sound at the centre of gravity of the speakers'
  positions in meters instead of their directions (Speaker Layout window ▸ Radiant places sound by). It shows how the
  feeds share the signal out over that layout; it is not a measured source distance.
- **Spatial window: Standard or Fast** (Soundstage settings ▸ Analysis and motion, Radiant). Fast measures the bands
  from 1 kHz up over shorter windows (about 43 ms, and 21 ms from 4 kHz) so their particles follow moves and onsets
  sooner, with coarser frequency detail. Standard, the full window, stays the default.
- **Raw observations** (same place): draws the newest analysis frame as it is, every particle small and alike, without
  smear, glow or exposure control, for checking where sound is placed.
- Particles are born on the analysed audio's own clock: a frame shown twice is never added twice, and a change of
  layout clears the old layout's particles instead of mixing them with the new ones.

## Smaller and faster in build 96

- The installed app is 44 % smaller (11.4 MB → 6.3 MB): its program no longer carries a symbol table that nothing
  reads at run time. The Bridge plugins are about 40 % smaller for the same reason, and the installer 15 % smaller
  (5.6 MB → 4.8 MB).
- About 9 % less main-thread work while metering: the VU needle moves on the meters' own display refresh instead of a

- Unused code removed.
- The measurements were reviewed against ITU-R BS.1770-5 (K-weighting at every sample rate, gating, channel weights,
  true peak), EBU Tech 3342 (loudness range) and IEC 60268-17 (VU ballistics). No reading changes in this build.

## Build 92

### New

- **Analyze Audio File… (⌘O, Meter menu).** Measures a whole file (WAV, AIFF, CAF, MP3, AAC/M4A, ALAC, FLAC and the
  rest of what macOS decodes) with the same analysis as live metering and the Bridge plugins, at the file's own sample
  rate. Mono, stereo and multichannel files from 3.0 to 9.1.6 (SMPTE channel order). The result window shows the
  verdict, the headline readings and each section's verdict. You can judge the file against any delivery standard
  without changing the meter's, then save the PDF report or the CSV. You can also drop a file on the window. A
  five-minute file takes about two seconds.
- **Redesigned loudness & master report.** Four A4 pages with the Auraliscope header and page numbers on each page:
  - page 1: summary, verdict, readings, section overview and loudness over time
  - page 2: loudness, dynamics and stereo image
  - page 3: average spectrum, tonal balance and bandwidth
  - page 4: signal integrity and how to read the report

  The same report is used for live sessions (⌘E) and for files.

### Changed

- The DAW Bridge line that was tested as Auraliscope LABS is now Auraliscope itself, replacing Auraliscope 2.0
  (build 91) and its Bridge plugins in place.
- The name is Auraliscope everywhere: menus, About, window titles, the report, and the plugin window, which now has a
  BETA badge. The "Prototype experiment only" notes are gone.
- The app uses Auraliscope's own identity (`com.jinboz.SoundMeterJBZ`) and installs as `/Applications/Auraliscope.app`.
  Auraliscope's settings stay as they are. On first launch the beta takes over any Auraliscope LABS setting that
  Auraliscope never stored, such as layouts, panel settings and theme. Logs are in `~/Library/Logs/Auraliscope`.
- The Bridge plugins (now version 2.0.93) connect on the 2.0 socket (`bridge-v2.sock`). Auraliscope LABS and its
  plugins keep working on their own, side by side, until you remove them.

## Checks

- File analysis against EBU Tech 3341 case 1 reads −23.0 LUFS, the same as a tone on one channel (−26.0) and on a
  5.1 surround channel (−24.5). An AAC copy reads the same, and a damaged file is refused (`scripts/check-file-analysis.sh`).
- On real MP3, AIFF and WAV files, integrated loudness is within 0.05 LU of an independent BS.1770 implementation.
- 57 core checks, the kernel checks and the localization checks pass (English, Chinese, Korean).
- Strip and panel transitions are measured by `--test-strip-return` (refresh gaps, CPU, and whether the toolbar comes
  back clear of the window buttons).
